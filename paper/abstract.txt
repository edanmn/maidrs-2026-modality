A near-chance baseline is usually read as evidence that a modality carries no signal. For LINCS L1000 transcriptomics it is more often evidence about the pipeline. Averaged across all doses, cell lines and timepoints and fed to a random forest, drug-level expression recovers pharmacological class at AUROC 0.548 on Phase 2 (95% CI [0.520, 0.577]), close enough to chance to read as absence. That averaging cancels 64% of signature magnitude, and tree ensembles suit dense high-dimensional features poorly. With both corrected, expression reaches 0.752 [0.728, 0.776], and the corrected features recover known pharmacology unsupervised, placing HMGCR, the direct target of every statin, among the top ten of 978 landmark genes (q ≤ 0.018). An independent release (LINCS Phase 1, 46,726 signatures) separates the general half of this diagnosis from the dataset-specific half. Model-class mismatch costs roughly 0.14 AUROC in seven of eight release-by-representation combinations, while the aggregation effect reverses sign between releases and has to be settled per dataset. All of this came out of a negative control that failed decisively. A CLIP-style structure-expression aligner lost to a random forest on ECFP4 fingerprints in every class (p ≤ 0.027), as did four stronger structural baselines and the published alignment objective, at 0.751 against 0.917 (p ≤ 0.0039). Correctly specified, expression is still redundant with structure for these tasks (+0.001, p = 0.15). A self-audit reports five defects in our own pipeline, each of which produced a publishable-looking but incorrect number.
